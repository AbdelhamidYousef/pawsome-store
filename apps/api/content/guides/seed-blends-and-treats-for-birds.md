A bowl of seed looks like the obvious bird food, and for many small cage birds it is a fine foundation. But seed alone is a bit like living on breakfast cereal: tasty, filling and missing quite a lot. With a few simple habits you can give your budgie, canary or finch a varied, balanced diet that keeps feathers bright and energy levels high.

## Know what your bird eats in nature

Different birds need different menus, so start with the species.

- **Budgies and other small parakeets** eat grass seeds in the wild, plus shoots and greens. They do well on a quality seed mix combined with pellets and fresh vegetables.
- **Canaries and finches** are seed eaters too, but they appreciate soft food, greens and the odd bit of egg food, especially during moulting.
- **Larger parrots** such as cockatiels and conures need more variety again, and many avian vets suggest pellets as the main part of the diet.

If you are not sure what suits your bird, ask an avian vet or the breeder for the current recommendation for that species.

## Choosing a good seed mix

A good blend is clean, fresh and suited to the size of your bird's beak. Look for:

- Seeds that look plump and glossy, not dusty, dull or clumped together
- A mix of several seed types rather than mostly one filler seed
- A clear best-before date, and a bag you can close properly

Our Garden Seed Blend is made for small cage birds and works well as the seed part of the menu. Store seed in an airtight container in a cool, dry cupboard. If it smells musty, shows webbing or has tiny moving dots, throw it away.

One classic trap: birds husk their seeds and leave the empty shells in the bowl. A bowl can look full when it is mostly husks. Blow the shells off the top every day, or simply empty and refill it.

## Add pellets, greens and a little fruit

Seed is high in fat and low in some vitamins and minerals. You can fill the gaps with:

- **Pellets**, introduced slowly alongside the usual seed. Many birds are suspicious of new food, so be patient; it can take weeks.
- **Fresh vegetables** every day: leafy greens, grated carrot, broccoli, peppers, courgette. Wash everything well.
- **Fruit** as a smaller extra, such as apple (without the pips), berries or melon, because it is high in sugar.
- **A source of minerals**, such as a cuttlebone or mineral block, which many small birds nibble on happily.

Clip greens to the cage bars or offer them on a separate dish, and remove fresh food after a few hours so it doesn't spoil.

## Treats: small, special and useful

Treats are great for bonding and training. A millet spray is the favourite of many budgies and finches, and our Millet Spray Treats are a simple way to offer one. Snap off a short piece rather than hanging up the whole spray, and keep treats to a small part of the day's food.

Use treats on purpose: to tempt a shy bird onto your hand, to reward stepping onto a perch, or to make a new toy or feeder interesting. A treat that's always there stops being special.

## Foods that are never safe

Some foods are toxic to birds, even in small amounts. Never offer:

- Avocado, in any form
- Chocolate, coffee, tea or anything with caffeine
- Alcohol, and very salty, sugary or fatty snacks
- Onion, garlic, and fruit pits or apple pips

Fumes matter too. Non-stick cookware that overheats can release gases that are deadly to birds within minutes, so keep birds well away from the kitchen and never leave a non-stick pan heating unattended. Aerosols, scented candles and smoke are best avoided near the cage as well.

## Water and routine

Fresh water every day is non-negotiable. Wash bowls and drinkers daily, because seed and droppings soon end up in them. Feed at roughly the same times each day and watch how much your bird eats; you'll quickly learn what "normal" looks like.

A bird that eats less, fluffs up and sits quietly, or shows changes in its droppings may be unwell. Birds hide illness well, so don't wait: call an avian vet the same day.

## Quick checklist

- A fresh, species-appropriate seed mix, with husks cleared daily
- Pellets introduced slowly, plus fresh vegetables every day
- Fruit and millet as small extras, not the main meal
- No avocado, chocolate, caffeine or alcohol, and no non-stick fumes
- Clean water daily, and an avian vet if your bird seems off
