A small aquarium is one of the most relaxing things you can have at home. It's also a little living ecosystem, and the most common beginner mistake is moving too fast: setting up the tank on Saturday and adding fish on Sunday. Give it a few weeks and a little patience, and you'll have clear water and healthy fish for years.

## Choose the tank and the spot

Bigger tanks are actually easier to keep stable, because the water changes more slowly. For a first tank, around 20 litres is a sensible minimum for a few small fish or shrimp. Our Starter Tank 20 L comes as a set, so you're not hunting for parts.

Where you put it matters as much as what's in it:

- **A sturdy, level surface.** Water weighs about a kilo per litre, so a 20-litre tank weighs well over 20 kg when full.
- **Out of direct sunlight**, which heats the water and feeds algae.
- **Away from radiators and draughts**, and near a power socket.
- **Somewhere you'll enjoy it**, but not right next to a door that slams.

## Set up the basics

Rinse the gravel or sand in plain water (no soap) until it runs clear. Add it to the tank, then place decor, rocks and plants. Fish feel much safer with places to hide; our Silk Plant Pack gives quick cover without any upkeep, and you can mix in a few easy live plants later.

Fill the tank with tap water treated with a water conditioner, which removes chlorine. Set up the filter and, for tropical fish, the heater, and switch them on. Lighting such as our Soft Glow LED Bar shows off the tank beautifully; run it for around 8 hours a day on a timer, because more light mostly means more algae.

## Cycle the tank before fish arrive

This is the step that makes or breaks a new aquarium. Fish produce ammonia, which is toxic. In a mature tank, beneficial bacteria living mostly in the filter turn ammonia into nitrite, and then into the much less harmful nitrate. This is the **nitrogen cycle**, and a brand-new tank doesn't have those bacteria yet.

To build them up:

- Run the filter for **four to six weeks** before adding fish, with a small source of ammonia (a pinch of fish food every few days, or a bottled ammonia source made for aquariums).
- Use a liquid **water test kit** to track ammonia, nitrite and nitrate.
- The tank is ready when ammonia and nitrite both read zero and nitrate is starting to show.

A bottle of starter bacteria can speed things up, but testing is still the only way to know the tank is ready.

## Choose fish carefully and add them slowly

Small tanks suit small, peaceful species. Ask your aquatic shop which fish suit your tank size, your water and each other, and check how big they grow as adults.

- **Don't overstock.** Start with just a few fish, and plan the final community so you stay well within the tank's capacity.
- **Add fish in small groups**, a couple of weeks apart, so the bacteria can keep up.
- **Acclimatise** new fish: float the closed bag for about 15 minutes so the temperature evens out, then net the fish into the tank. Don't pour the shop water in.
- Some fish, such as many tetras and rasboras, need to live in groups to feel safe.

## Feeding and weekly care

Overfeeding is the second classic mistake. Feed only what your fish finish in a minute or two, once or twice a day. Leftover food rots and spoils the water.

A simple weekly routine keeps the tank healthy:

- Change **10 to 20 percent of the water** with conditioned water at the same temperature.
- Vacuum a section of the gravel to remove waste.
- Wipe algae off the glass.
- Rinse filter media only in old tank water, never under the tap, so you don't wash away the good bacteria.

## Watch for trouble

Healthy fish swim actively, eat eagerly and have clear eyes and fins. Gasping at the surface, clamped fins, white spots, or fish hiding and refusing food are all signs something is wrong. Test the water first, since most problems start there, and do a partial water change if anything reads high. If fish still seem unwell, ask an aquatic specialist or a vet with fish experience before adding any treatment.

## Quick checklist

- A stable spot out of the sun, with a sturdy surface
- Substrate rinsed, decor in, water conditioned, filter running
- Four to six weeks of cycling, confirmed with a test kit
- A few small, compatible fish at a time; no overstocking
- Feed lightly and change 10 to 20 percent of the water weekly
