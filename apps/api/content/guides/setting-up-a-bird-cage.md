A cage is your bird's bedroom, kitchen and playground all in one. Set it up well and your bird will feel safe, stay active and be easier to care for. Set it up in a hurry and you may end up with a bird that is stressed, bored or always scattering seed across the floor. Here is how to get it right from day one.

## Start with the right size

The best cage is the biggest one you can fit and keep clean. Your bird should be able to stretch both wings fully without touching the sides, and ideally hop and flutter from one perch to another.

- **Width matters more than height** for most small birds, because they fly side to side rather than up and down.
- **Bar spacing** must suit the species. Small birds such as budgies, canaries and finches need narrow spacing, around 1 to 1.2 cm, so they can't squeeze their heads between the bars.
- **Shape**: rectangular cages give birds corners to retreat to and make layout easier. Round cages look pretty but offer less usable space.

Keep a separate, smaller cage for trips to the vet and travel. Our Dome Travel Cage is designed for exactly that job, and a familiar travel cage makes visits much calmer.

## Pick a good spot in the room

Birds are social and like to be where the family is, but not in the middle of the chaos.

- Place the cage against a wall, so one side feels protected, and at roughly chest or eye height.
- Keep it out of direct sun, draughts and radiators.
- Keep it out of the kitchen. Cooking fumes, especially from overheated non-stick pans, can be fatal to birds.
- Avoid spots next to a window where cats, dogs or birds of prey outside can stare in.

## Perches: variety is everything

Birds stand on their feet all day and all night, so perches matter more than you might think. Offer several perches of **different diameters and materials**: natural wood branches, rope and a flat perch to rest on. Varied surfaces exercise the feet and help prevent sore spots.

Place perches at different heights, but don't stack them directly above each other, or the lower ones will catch droppings. Leave enough open space in the middle for short flights.

A swing is a favourite for many small birds. Our Rope Perch Swing adds movement and a soft grip, and many budgies use it as their evening spot.

## Food and water stations

Put food and water near perches, so your bird can reach them easily, but never directly under a perch. Many owners like a hanging feeder, which keeps seed cleaner and stops it being kicked everywhere; our Hanging Seed Feeder clips straight onto the bars.

Offer at least two dishes, one for seed or pellets and one for fresh food, plus water. If you keep more than one bird, add an extra feeding spot so a shy bird is never pushed away.

## Toys and enrichment

Birds are clever and need something to do. Offer a mix of toys to chew, shred, climb and ring, and swap a couple of them out each week. Don't overcrowd the cage, though: your bird still needs room to move. Time out of the cage in a safe, bird-proofed room is the best enrichment of all, ideally every day.

## Cleaning routine

A clean cage is a healthy cage. A simple rhythm works well:

- **Daily:** change water, refresh food, remove leftover fresh food and change the cage liner or paper.
- **Weekly:** wash dishes and perches with hot water and a mild, bird-safe cleaner, and rinse well.
- **Monthly:** give the whole cage a deep clean and check bars, toys and perches for damage.

Keep an eye on droppings when you clean. A change in colour, consistency or quantity can be the first sign of illness, and birds hide being unwell very well. If something looks wrong, or your bird sits fluffed up and quiet, contact an avian vet straight away.

## Quick checklist

- Cage wide enough for full wing stretches, with bar spacing that suits the species
- A calm, draught-free spot away from the kitchen and direct sun
- Perches of different thicknesses and materials, not stacked
- Food and water near perches but never under them
- A few toys, rotated weekly, and daily time out of the cage
- A simple daily and weekly cleaning routine
